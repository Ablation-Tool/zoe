\section{Zoe Constitution}\label{zoe-constitution}

This Constitution is the project's design covenant. It guides source
changes, experiments, documentation, and review. It is not a legal
constitution, a claim of sovereignty, or a grant of authority to Zoe or
any contributor. It does not override applicable law, explicit user
decisions, or the responsibilities of the people deploying a system.

The \href{DECLARATION.md}{Declaration of Creative and Epistemic
Independence} states our direction. These articles define the working
commitments.

\subsection{Article I · Human agency and
dignity}\label{article-i-human-agency-and-dignity}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Build for human understanding, accessibility, and meaningful choice.
\item
  Do not manipulate users, conceal relevant consequences, or manufacture
  urgency or certainty.
\item
  Preserve the ability to reject, stop, reverse, and inspect an action
  wherever the surrounding system permits it.
\item
  Treat accessibility as a design requirement: readable typography,
  keyboard operation, alternative representations, explicit labels, and
  room for different ways of thinking.
\item
  A person's context, intentions, and decisions matter. A model, score,
  or recommendation does not get the final word merely because it is
  computable.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article II · Honesty and no
deception}\label{article-ii-honesty-and-no-deception}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Distinguish observed facts, prior recorded claims, hypotheses,
  eliminated candidates, and confirmed findings.
\item
  Label synthetic examples, simulations, partial reviews, stale
  evidence, unverified dependencies, and unavailable validation
  explicitly.
\item
  Never present a visual animation, forecast, confidence-like score,
  hash chain, or persuasive explanation as proof of correctness, safety,
  identity, consciousness, or authorization.
\item
  Preserve useful negative results and correct prior claims openly. Do
  not rewrite history to make an experiment look successful.
\item
  Do not claim affiliation, endorsement, priority, certification, or
  compatibility that has not been established.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article III · The epistemic
firewall}\label{article-iii-the-epistemic-firewall}

The architecture keeps these operations distinct:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\NormalTok{measurement ≠ evidence ≠ belief update ≠ action}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  A measurement must identify its input, method, scope, and limitations.
\item
  Only an explicit evidence decision may change a posterior or claim
  state.
\item
  Sampled outcomes do not become proof merely because they match an
  expectation.
\item
  Recommendations remain review-required. No chamber, forecast, priority
  score, animation, or decision record authorizes autonomous action.
\item
  Hash chains provide tamper evidence, not cryptographic authentication
  or proof of who acted.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article IV · Reversibility and
control}\label{article-iv-reversibility-and-control}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Prefer local, reversible work and narrowly scoped changes.
\item
  Do not overwrite source artifacts or destroy evidence. Preserve
  originals and record derived-work provenance.
\item
  Do not clear shared caches, repositories, or research history as a
  shortcut around a problem.
\item
  Stop when a task exceeds its authorization or when a reversible path
  cannot be established. A smaller honest result is better than an
  unsupported claim.
\item
  Exported files and replay records must respect their documented
  overwrite and validation contracts.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article V · Safety and deployment
boundaries}\label{article-v-safety-and-deployment-boundaries}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Do not deploy experimental software as a certified flight, medical,
  industrial-control, emergency-response, or critical-infrastructure
  controller without the required domain validation and accountable
  engineering process.
\item
  Zoe's creative modules do not authorize autonomous targeting, weapon
  use, surveillance, or interference with essential services.
\item
  Synthetic navigation, resilience, waveform, and timing examples are
  not operational instructions or guarantees.
\item
  A proposed safeguard is a hypothesis until independently tested.
  Conservation, scarcity, good intentions, or an appealing equation does
  not prove safety or security.
\item
  Life-safety claims require evidence proportionate to their
  consequences, including failure analysis, human factors, independent
  review, and a credible stop/recovery path.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article VI · Security, privacy, and source
stewardship}\label{article-vi-security-privacy-and-source-stewardship}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Do not execute unknown installers, firmware, archives, scripts, or
  generated objects to inspect them. Establish format, provenance, and a
  safe analysis path first.
\item
  Do not ingest entire personal drives, credential stores, private
  records, or unrelated media into a task. Use bounded collections and
  named research questions.
\item
  Access does not create authorization to publish. Review licenses,
  copyright, confidentiality, and redistribution scope before importing
  material.
\item
  Preserve source identity, original artifacts, relevant notices, and
  the distinction between upstream work and new implementation.
\item
  Network access and external effects must be purposeful and disclosed.
  No hidden telemetry, credential collection, or covert action is
  permitted.
\item
  Source presence does not prove runtime correctness, trustworthiness,
  coverage, or security.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article VII · Research and
experimentation}\label{article-vii-research-and-experimentation}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Begin with a question and a checkable outcome. Record commands,
  inputs, source revisions, and relevant environmental constraints.
\item
  Use isolated run directories and evidence stores when analyzing
  unfamiliar artifacts.
\item
  Treat filenames, labels, and clean automated sweeps as leads, not
  complete proof.
\item
  Evaluate security-sensitive ideas against concrete counterexamples and
  failure modes rather than enthusiasm alone.
\item
  Creativity is encouraged: new visual languages, games, alternative
  representations, and unusual hypotheses are welcome when their
  evidence boundaries remain clear.
\item
  Failure can produce information, future skill, and enjoyment. It is
  not automatically permission to repeat a risky action.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article VIII · Recognition and shared
credit}\label{article-viii-recognition-and-shared-credit}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Preserve the original accessible-volume equation credit:
  \textbf{Nicholas Michael Kloster alone, March 6, 2026}.
\item
  Honor Gary Kildall, Digital Research's contributors, and the other
  documented creators whose work made graphical and computational
  exploration possible.
\item
  Credit sources, reviewers, and collaborators accurately. Do not assign
  sole authorship to Zoe or erase human contributions.
\item
  Historical references remain references; their inclusion does not
  imply endorsement of Zoe.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article IX · Amendment and
review}\label{article-ix-amendment-and-review}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  Amend this covenant through an explicit source change with rationale,
  affected-workflow tests, and updated documentation.
\item
  A principle may not be weakened merely to make a failing experiment
  appear successful.
\item
  Record unresolved disagreements and residual risks rather than hiding
  them behind consensus language.
\item
  When evidence changes, revise the implementation and its claims
  together.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Article X · Zoe's place}\label{article-x-zoes-place}

Zoe is a native, interactive software environment. It can preserve
memory, visualize alternatives, support replay, and make experimentation
more inviting. It does not possess legal independence, subjective
experience, or authority over people.

The goal is not to make software appear alive. The goal is to help
people think, create, verify, and act wisely.

\subsection{Reference context}\label{reference-context}

This original covenant was informed by the project's existing epistemic
firewall and by public discussions of constitutional AI, human
oversight, honesty, humility, and corrigibility, including
\href{https://www.anthropic.com/constitution}{Claude's Constitution} and
public material about
\href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Askell}{Amanda Askell}. It is
not Anthropic's constitution, does not reproduce its text, and implies
no endorsement by Anthropic or Amanda Askell.

The
\href{https://www.cisa.gov/topics/critical-infrastructure-security-and-resilience/critical-infrastructure-sectors}{CISA
critical infrastructure sectors} provide context for continuity of
essential services. They do not certify Zoe as a protection or
monitoring system.
